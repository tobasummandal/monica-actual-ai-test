# Adopt Domain-Driven Service Layer Pattern for Journal Management: Domain Operations Organized

## Status
Proposed

## Context
Journal management operations require clear organizational boundaries to maintain separation of concerns and enable scalable domain logic. Current architecture lacks explicit service layer patterns that organize domain operations by subdomain.

## Decision
Adopt a domain-driven service layer pattern for journal management where domain operations are organized by subdomain (ManageJournals) with clear namespace boundaries separating Web, Services, and other concerns.

## Policies

1. Domain operations MUST be organized by subdomain (ManageJournals) with clear namespace boundaries separating Web, Services, and other concerns

## Rationale
- Establishes explicit boundaries between presentation, service, and domain layers
- Enables independent evolution of domain logic within subdomains
- Improves testability through clear separation of concerns
- Facilitates team ownership of specific subdomains
- Reduces coupling between web handlers and business logic

## Consequences

### Positive
- Clear organizational structure aligned with business subdomains
- Easier to locate and modify domain operations
- Improved code reusability across different web handlers
- Better support for domain-driven design principles

### Negative
- Additional abstraction layer may introduce slight performance overhead
- Requires discipline to maintain namespace boundaries
- Team must understand and follow subdomain organization patterns

## Implementation Notes
- Service layer classes should reside in `Services` namespace under subdomain
- Web handlers should delegate to service layer rather than implementing logic directly
- Each subdomain service should have single responsibility
- Cross-subdomain operations should be coordinated at application service level
