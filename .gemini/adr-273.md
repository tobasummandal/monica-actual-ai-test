# Adopt Domain-Driven Service Layer Pattern for Journal Management: Services Composed Together

## Status
Proposed

## Context
Journal management requires handling complex workflows that span multiple business domains. A service layer pattern enables clean separation of concerns while allowing services to be composed together for sophisticated operations.

## Decision
Adopt a domain-driven service layer pattern where services are composed together to implement complex journal management workflows. Each service encapsulates domain logic for a specific bounded context and can be independently tested and reused.

## Consequences

### Positive
- Services remain focused on single responsibilities
- Complex workflows are built through composition rather than monolithic implementations
- Each service can be tested in isolation
- Services are reusable across different workflows
- Clear domain boundaries improve maintainability

### Negative
- Service composition adds complexity to workflow orchestration
- Requires careful design to avoid circular dependencies
- Testing composed services requires integration test coverage

## Policies

1. Services MAY be composed together to implement complex workflows, but each service should remain independently testable

## Implementation Notes
- Define clear service contracts and interfaces
- Use dependency injection for service composition
- Maintain comprehensive unit tests for individual services
- Document service dependencies and composition patterns
