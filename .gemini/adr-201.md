# Adopt Eloquent ORM Models as Standard Data Access Layer: Business Logic Not

## Status
Accepted

## Context
Our application requires a consistent approach to data access and model organization. Eloquent ORM provides a powerful abstraction for database interactions within Laravel. However, without clear boundaries, models can become bloated with business logic, making them difficult to test, maintain, and evolve.

## Decision
We adopt Eloquent ORM Models as the standard data access layer for our application. Models will serve as the primary interface for database operations and data representation. Business logic will be separated from models and implemented in dedicated service classes, repositories, or action classes.

## Policies

1. Business logic MUST NOT be tightly coupled to model classes; models should focus on data representation and persistence

## Consequences

### Positive
- Clear separation of concerns between data access and business logic
- Improved testability of business logic independent of database operations
- Easier maintenance and evolution of models
- Consistent data access patterns across the application
- Reduced model complexity and cognitive load

### Negative
- Additional abstraction layers may introduce slight performance overhead
- Developers must maintain discipline in adhering to the separation
- Requires more files and classes for feature implementation

## Implementation Guidelines

- Use Eloquent models exclusively for database queries and relationships
- Implement business logic in Service classes or Action classes
- Use Repository pattern if additional abstraction is needed
- Keep model methods focused on data transformation and relationships
- Avoid conditional logic and external service calls in models
