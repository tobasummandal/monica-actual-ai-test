# Adopt Domain-Driven DAV/CardDAV Integration Pattern for External Contact Synchronization: External Dav Carddav

## Status
Proposed

## Context
External contact synchronization via DAV/CardDAV protocols requires integration with third-party calendar and contact management systems. Without a clear architectural pattern, DAV/CardDAV client logic risks being scattered across multiple layers, leading to tight coupling, reduced testability, and difficulty in maintaining protocol-specific concerns.

## Decision
Adopt a domain-driven integration pattern where all DAV/CardDAV interactions are encapsulated within domain-specific service layers. The Contact domain will own a dedicated DavClient service responsible for all protocol-level operations, authentication, and data transformation.

## Policies

1. External DAV/CardDAV integrations MUST be encapsulated within domain-specific service layers (e.g., Contact domain DavClient services)

## Consequences

### Positive
- Protocol concerns isolated to a single, testable service layer
- Domain logic remains independent of DAV/CardDAV implementation details
- Easier to swap or upgrade DAV/CardDAV libraries without affecting domain logic
- Clear separation of concerns between domain and infrastructure
- Simplified mocking and unit testing of contact synchronization workflows

### Negative
- Additional service layer introduces minor architectural overhead
- Requires careful design to avoid leaking protocol details into domain models
- Team must maintain discipline in keeping DAV/CardDAV logic confined to designated services

## Alternatives Considered
- Embedding DAV/CardDAV logic directly in domain entities (rejected: violates separation of concerns)
- Centralizing all external integrations in a generic integration layer (rejected: loses domain context)
